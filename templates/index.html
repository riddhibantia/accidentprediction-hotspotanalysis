Data insights

What the records show

National patterns in collision frequency, and the conditions that shape injury severity.

Six national patterns, one at a time. Step through with Back and Next below, the arrow keys, or the progress bar — each step explains exactly what its figures show.

Weather

Casualties by weather

How to read it — each bar is the number of casualties recorded under one weather type, across all 2023 collisions. A taller bar means more people were hurt in that weather, not a higher chance of being hurt: fine weather leads simply because most driving happens in it. The risk signal is the share of those casualties that were serious or fatal, which rises in rain, fog and snow.

Select a bar to send that weather to the severity predictor.

Time of day

Collisions by time of day

How to read it — the line plots how many collisions started in each three-hour window of the day; the shaded area is the same figure filled in for readability. The two peaks follow the morning and evening commutes, when the most vehicles are on the road. The small hours look quiet because far fewer people are driving, not because each trip is safer. Read this chart as volume of collisions, and use the severity mix for how dangerous they are.

Road surface

Casualties by road surface

How to read it — each slice is the number of casualties recorded on that road surface, and the slice area is proportional to that count. Dry surfaces dominate because dry conditions are the norm. The figure worth reading is severity, not volume: wet, icy and snowy surfaces produce a larger share of serious and fatal outcomes.

Select a slice to send that surface to the severity predictor.

Speed limit

High-severity share by speed limit

How to read it — each bar is the percentage of casualties on that posted speed limit that were killed or seriously injured, on a 0 to 40 scale. The share climbs sharply from 20 mph up to 60 mph. The 70 mph bar sits lower, which mostly reflects where that limit applies: motorways, where traffic is separated and junctions are rare. This is the clearest single risk gradient in the data.

Severity mix

Severity mix through the day

How to read it — each bar is a three-hour window and the three segments show how that window's casualties split between slight, serious and fatal. Every bar totals 100%, so the windows can be compared by mix even though they differ in volume. Slight shrinks and fatal grows overnight: fewer collisions, but a higher proportion of them are violent.

Vehicle type

High-severity share by vehicle type

How to read it — each bar is one vehicle type, labelled with the number of casualty records it covers (n). The two segments are that type's serious and fatal shares, so the bar length is its high-severity rate. Motorcyclists, cyclists and HGVs carry a much larger serious-or-fatal share than cars, which dominate the raw count but rank near the bottom. Types with a small n should be read with care.
